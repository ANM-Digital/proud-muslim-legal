Advanced Prayer Times — Complete Guide

A practical reference for the additional time markers shown on Proud Muslim’s Advanced Prayer Times screen. Prayer-time terminology and legal rulings can vary by school and calculation convention; users should follow trusted local religious guidance where a ruling differs.

İmsak (Fecr-i Sâdık)

İmsak marks true dawn (al-fajr al-sadiq), when horizontal dawn light spreads across the eastern horizon. It begins Fajr time and the fasting boundary. Qur’an 2:187 describes this dawn distinction.

Sabah Kerahati

The morning restricted-prayer period around sunrise. Precise rulings and exceptions differ among schools; the app warning is a fiqh alert rather than a universal ruling for every prayer.

Sabah Namazı

Fajr prayer begins at true dawn and ends at sunrise. It is one of the five obligatory daily prayers.

Güneş

Sunrise ends the normal Fajr window. Prayer at the actual moment of sunrise is among the restricted times described in hadith.

İşrak

A post-sunrise voluntary-prayer marker after the immediate sunrise restriction has passed, commonly associated with Salat al-Ishraq.

Dahve-i Kübrâ

A traditional later-morning fiqh/timekeeping marker. In Hanafi fasting discussions it is relevant to intention for certain fasts, subject to applicable conditions.

Öğle Kerahat Vakti

The short restricted period around solar culmination. Once the sun passes the meridian, Dhuhr time begins.

Öğle Namazı

Dhuhr begins after the sun passes the local meridian and continues until Asr begins according to the selected juristic shadow criterion.

İkindi Namazı

Asr is the obligatory afternoon prayer. Its start is determined using shadow length; the commonly applied Hanafi calculation differs from the criterion commonly used for Shafi‘i, Maliki and Hanbali timetables.

İkindi Kerahati

A late-afternoon restriction alert as sunset approaches. Treatment of missed obligatory and exceptional prayers differs among juristic schools.

Asr-ı Sânî

“Second Asr” is the later Asr threshold associated with the two-shadow-factor calculation and is especially useful where both Asr criteria are displayed.

İsfirâr

The late-afternoon stage when the sun weakens/yellows toward the horizon, discussed in classical fiqh in connection with delaying Asr and restricted late-afternoon prayer.

Akşam Namazı

Maghrib begins after sunset. Its ending boundary is related to the disappearance of twilight, with juristic/calculation details differing.

İştibâk

A traditional twilight-related marker found in detailed Islamic timetables; it is not a separate obligatory prayer.

Yatsı Namazı

Isha begins with the disappearance of evening twilight. Red/white twilight criteria and high-latitude handling can differ among methods.

İşâ-i Sânî

“Second Isha” is a later twilight marker found in detailed traditional timetables and may represent an alternative or more conservative twilight criterion.

Gece Yarısı Vakti

Islamic midnight in prayer-time discussions is generally derived by dividing the night interval rather than simply using civil 00:00. Its legal significance differs by juristic discussion.

Teheccüd Namazı

Voluntary night prayer performed after Isha. The final portion of the night is especially associated with worship, supplication and seeking forgiveness.

Seher Vakti (Fecr-i Kâzib)

The pre-dawn part of night. False dawn is an earlier light phenomenon distinguished in the Prophetic tradition from the horizontal true dawn that begins Fajr.

Kıble Sâati Vakti

A date- and location-dependent solar alignment time used to help determine Qibla direction. It is not a prayer time. Its exact use depends on the Qibla-time calculation method and must be calculated separately from the five daily prayers.

Sources and methodology

This guide summarizes prayer-time concepts found in the Qur’an, canonical hadith literature and classical Islamic jurisprudence. Detailed rulings vary among Hanafi, Shafi‘i, Maliki, Hanbali and Ja‘fari jurisprudence.

Important: Proud Muslim provides prayer-time information as a practical aid. For disputed fiqh questions, unusual latitudes, fasting rulings or circumstances requiring a legal ruling, consult a qualified local scholar or recognized religious authority.
